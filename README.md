# Bangkok Heat Map Prototype

**Question:** Which Bangkok districts are hottest, why, and who is most exposed?

An interactive Streamlit map with three layers: land surface temperature (LST), heat drivers, and a
heat-risk score for all 50 districts (เขต). All numbers below are generated by `python -m src.report`
from the processed files; nothing is typed in by hand.

```
streamlit run app/streamlit_app.py
```

## Data sources
| Data | Source |
|---|---|
| District boundaries | data.bangkok.go.th, "พื้นที่เขตปกครอง 50 เขต" |
| Land surface temperature, NDVI | Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 Level-2 via Google Earth Engine |
| Built-up fraction | ESA WorldCover v200 (2021, 10 m) via Google Earth Engine |
| Parks, water, buildings | OpenStreetMap (BBBike Bangkok extract plus Overpass for the eastern strip) |
| Air temperature | Open-Meteo historical API (ERA5-based) |
| Validation stations | TMD stations; coordinates from the NOAA ISD station list |
| Elderly population | data.bangkok.go.th elderly-allowance recipients (Sep 2566 = Sep 2023) |
| Registered population | BMA district boundary dataset attributes |

## Method
1. **LST.** Landsat 8 and 9 scenes, March–May of 2023, 2024, 2025, cloud-masked with `QA_PIXEL`; `ST_B10` converted
   to °C; median composite at 30 m.
2. **Drivers.** NDVI from the same composite; green-space %, mean distance to water and OSM building
   counts per district. LST and NDVI district means are **land-only** (open water excluded using OSM water
   polygons and NDVI < 0), because water is cool and has very low NDVI and otherwise hides the
   LST-NDVI relationship.
3. **Validation.** Landsat LST (3×3-pixel mean) against Open-Meteo 2 m air temperature at five TMD stations,
   using the March–May median of values interpolated to 10:30.
4. **Score.** `heat_risk = z(LST) + z(built-up %) − z(NDVI) + z(elderly %)`, z-scores across the 50 districts.

## Where to add green space (sub-district analysis)
A 250 m grid gives each cell LST, NDVI, built-up share and WorldPop 2020 population. Priority =
z(LST) + z(built-up %) − z(NDVI) + z(log population density). **Hotspots** are connected clusters
(at least two cells) in the top 15% of priority that are more than 400 m from any existing green zone.
**Existing green zones** are OSM parks plus open satellite green: WorldCover tree/shrub/grass patches of at least
2 ha with NDVI ≥ 0.45 and less than 40% built-up land in a 60 m ring around them (this excludes cropland).
Tree canopy inside housing areas is kept as a separate, uncounted layer. An earlier version counted it, and it
turned out to be 53% of "green" area, so green space was overstated; the map is now stricter.

- 183 hotspots cover 8,200 ha and about 1,049,183 people.
- 36 of 50 districts have less than 9 m² of green space per person (the commonly cited WHO minimum).
- Least green space per person: Wang Thong Lang (0.4 m²), Samphanthawong (0.4 m²), Pom Prap Sattru Phai (0.5 m²), Thon buri (0.5 m²), Phasi Charoen (0.6 m²).

## Limitations
- Landsat thermal bands have a native resolution of 100 m (resampled to 30 m in Collection 2).
- The overpass is around 10:30 am local time and says nothing about afternoon or night heat.
- The composite is a March–May seasonal median across 3 years, not a single heat-wave day.
- **OSM coverage varies by district.** Building counts are too low in many dense districts: for example Thon buri has
  1,294 OSM buildings in 8.5 km² (5.1% building share vs 86% built-up in WorldCover),
  so OSM building share is unreliable. Districts flagged below 25% of median building density: Bang Khae, Thawi Watthana, Min Buri, Lat Krabang, Bang Khun thain, Nong Chok.
  Built-up share from ESA WorldCover is used in the score instead.
- Built-up share and NDVI are strongly correlated, so the score partly counts land cover twice.
- Elderly share is a proxy: BMA allowance recipients divided by registered population, not a census age
  breakdown. Population and elderly counts are from different years.
- Validation has only five stations, and Open-Meteo is a coarse reanalysis grid.
- **OSM parks and gardens are missing east of about 100.858°E** (parts of Nong Chok and Lat Krabang): the BBBike
  extract stops there and the Overpass fill-in kept failing, so only satellite open green covers that strip.
- The eastern edge of the city is outside the BBBike OSM extract and was filled from Overpass, so a few
  features near the seam may be counted twice in building counts.
- Hotspots depend on thresholds (top 15% priority, 400 m, 1 ha) that are judgement calls. WorldPop 2020 is a modelled
  population grid and differs from registered population. Green zones under 2 ha (street trees, pocket parks) and canopy inside housing are not
  counted, so green per person is a lower bound. Hotspot locations are screening results, not site assessments.
- The score is a simple index that has not been validated against health outcomes.

## Deploy
The app reads only the small processed files committed under `data/`. Run locally with `streamlit run app/streamlit_app.py`
or deploy to Streamlit Community Cloud with main file `app/streamlit_app.py` (it installs `requirements.txt`).
